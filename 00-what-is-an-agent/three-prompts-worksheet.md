# Three prompts worksheet

Write three prompts for **one** task. You will run all three in Module 1 and
compare the answers. This page shows a filled-in example first, then a blank
form for you.

## The three kinds

| Kind | What it has | What usually happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Vague** | Just the topic | The agent guesses what you want, or asks you questions |
| **Clear** | The topic, what you want, and the shape of the answer | You get roughly what you asked for |
| **Clear plus example** | All of the clear prompt, plus a sample of the answer | The answer matches your sample's style and length |

## Filled-in example

Task: plan dinners for a week.

**1. Vague**

```text
Dinner ideas.
```

**2. Clear**

```text
Give me five easy dinner ideas for weeknights. Each one should take under 30 minutes and use no oven. Answer as a numbered list, one line each.
```

**3. Clear plus example**

```text
Give me five easy dinner ideas for weeknights. Each one should take under 30 minutes and use no oven. Answer as a numbered list, one line each, like this example:

1. Egg fried rice - 15 min - eggs, rice, peas, soy sauce
```

## Your turn

Pick a small task from your own life. Good choices: planning a trip day,
writing a short thank-you note, making a study plan, or naming a new folder
system. Avoid anything private, such as passwords or bank details.

Copy this into a text file, or onto paper, and fill it in:

```text
My task:

1. Vague prompt:

2. Clear prompt:
   (say what you want, any limits, and the shape of the answer)

3. Clear prompt plus example:
   (the clear prompt, then "like this example:" and one sample line)
```

## Tips

- **Say the shape.** "A numbered list", "two sentences" or "a table with two
  columns".
- **Say the limits.** "Under 30 minutes", "no more than 100 words".
- **Keep the example short.** One line or one row is enough.
